# Curation record: 2005–2017

Thirty entries in five kairolexica were assembled from live-verified sources on
2026-10-11. Quality takes precedence over the initial 45–60-entry target. This
collection deliberately leaves big-data vocabulary sparse rather than treating
ordinary database terminology as short-lived jargon.

## Evidence and chronology

- Participatory-web principles: 8 entries. O'Reilly (2005) and Fowler (2006)
  attest the vocabulary; TechCrunch (2009) reports search growth through
  end-2007 and subsequent decline. Sterling's 2009 transcript criticizes the
  principles and documents lost novelty. The bounded promotional slogans are
  selected, not assertions that the technologies or practices stopped existing.
- Early Tumblr reaction captions: 7 entries. The July 5, 2012 Daily Dot guide
  is contemporaneous and expressly distinguishes its definitions from a parody
  guide. The platform's 2007 founding is not used to date later vernacular.
  Know Your Meme chronology supports the 2010–2012 emergence/spread of specific
  constructions. Parody documents backlash, but exact disappearance is unknown.
- Big-data promotional saturation: 2 entries. The January 2014 Guardian article
  expressly dates awareness breakthrough and backlash to 2013. KDnuggets (2015)
  records retirement from Gartner's chart; SiliconANGLE (2015) records parody.
  Neither implies that the valid technical concept disappeared. The service
  compound has a weaker, inferred decline and should be prioritized for review.
- Promotional workplace titles: 5 entries. Onrec reproduces Indeed observations
  in May 2016. Guru peaked in July 2012; wizard fluctuated after 2011; rockstar
  was in slight decline. The 2017 Conversation critique establishes professional
  backlash. Counterevidence is retained: HR Dive reports 90% ninja growth and
  17% rockstar growth in 2018. A universal death in 2016 is not defensible.
- Social-mobile promotional saturation: 8 entries. Period criticism and later
  retrospective rejection support growth hacking, snackable content, and SoLoMo.
  Collective Hub (2017) attests pitch-template saturation. The 2014/2015 public
  banishment lists document mainstream uptake/backlash for reaction phrases;
  they do not establish where community vocabulary originated.

All signature fade dates are marked estimated. Temporal parameters describe a
bounded promotional or caption register and are curation hypotheses. They are
not yearly frequency measurements, first-ever-use dates, or claims of extinction.
The single-peak schema cannot fully express the observed resurgence of job-title
suffixes. That limitation is recorded in the individual rationales rather than
concealed by moving dates.

## Source verification

Exact source URLs and access dates reside in each JSON record. Verification used
ordinary HTTPS through the inherited proxy, with TLS verification enabled.
Successful responses were read as text. The source article date was used where
shown; Know Your Meme pages are explicitly undated retrospective chronologies.
The Headhunters 2013 date is supported by the indexed record and adjacent
2013 article but is not displayed on the fetched page. WordStream's period URL
is retained with its later update date, since revised text cannot be assumed
identical to the original.

Attested examples are literal source phrases or excerpts, not fabricated quotes.
Constructed full utterances are explicitly marked constructed. Brief quotation
of a heading is evidence of lexical form, not independent evidence of everyday
spoken usage. Independent period utterance examples remain desirable.

Blocked sources were not treated as verified: the NYT article, Forbes articles,
Uloop guide, original Indeed blog pages, and Packt survey page returned access
failures. Search results were discovery aids, not substituted for unread articles.

## Candidates withheld

- Cinnamon roll and smol bean: strong 2014/2015 origin/spread evidence exists at
  Know Your Meme, but a clean fade/backlash interval was not established. Stage
  these for further research instead of calling present community usage dead.
- I am cry / my creys: period examples are discoverable, but insufficient direct
  source verification was obtained for both uptake and decline.
- Shipping, OTP, squee, and fandom: older and/or still functioning vocabulary;
  association with Tumblr does not by itself satisfy flare-and-die criteria.
- Hyperdata and mobile-born: the December 2013 VentureBeat article predicts
  growth. A prediction is not evidence that either term actually circulated.
- Data mining, Hadoop, NoSQL, MapReduce, the three Vs, and data visualization:
  technical vocabulary, without demonstrated short promotional lifespan here.
- Ninja, guru, and Jedi: accepted as workplace metaphors because attested rise,
  professional rejection, and bounded promotional phases are documented. Their
  social context and resurgence remain review considerations; no literal cultural
  identity or religious authority is being synthesized.
- Shaman: the Indeed release explicitly describes a 2014 flare followed by
  disappearance, but cultural sensitivity warrants Tom's review before inclusion.
- Cray-cray: a period banishment source exists, but the mental-health framing
  warrants Tom's review before inclusion.
- Open the kimono: withheld for cultural and sexualized connotations.

Bae and giving me life have older Black and queer speech histories. Their
signatures describe brief mainstream promotional uptake; these records do not
claim that the originating communities ceased using their own vocabulary.
