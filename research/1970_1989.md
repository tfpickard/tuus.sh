# 1970–1989 curation record

Reviewed 2026-10-11. Three kairolexica contain 28 accepted entries: citizens-band popularization (15), national Valley Girl lexical fad (12), and the yuppie demographic designation (1). This is below the research target because durable vocabulary and unsupported timestamps were not added to meet a count.

## Inspected evidence

- Gateway Records, *Everything You Need to Know to Operate a CB Radio* (1976): https://archive.org/details/everything-you-need-to-know-to-operate-a-cb-radio. Archive metadata and the high-resolution rear sleeve were inspected. The sleeve itself prints a 1976 copyright. Its dictionary and farewell section attest selected surfaces; its teaching text describes the words as commonly used. Headword presence is attestation of a form, not an observed spontaneous utterance. All CB examples are explicitly constructed applications of the documented meanings.
- Green Bay Press Gazette, *Ham Operators Ride Out CB Craze* (1976-10-10): https://archive.org/details/101076HamOperatorsRideOutCBCraze. Inspected the archived clipping's OCR. It documents a public craze and reports crowded-channel criticism and predicted fatigue. It does **not** prove the craze had already ended.
- CB World, *CB Radio Code, Lingo and Slang* (undated): https://www.wearecb.com/cb-radio-lingo.html. Inspected glossary and conversational guidance. This supports meanings and reveals continuing specialist use; it is not evidence that a word originated in 1976 or vanished in 1982.
- Michael Demarest, TIME, *Living: How Toe-dully Max Is Their Valley* (1982-09-27), page 1: https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,925750,00.html and page 2: https://content.time.com/time/subscriber/article/0,33009,925750-2,00.html. Both pages inspected. The first documents the Zappa record and national merchandising, while using the register itself. The second explicitly describes lexical ephemerality and reports that bitchen and tubular were already displaced Valley staples. Short attested examples preserve exact phrases; the longer intent applications are labeled constructed.
- *Valley girl*, Wikipedia raw source: https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Valley_girl&action=raw. Inspected source text. This supports the 1982 popularization of grody to the max and gag me with a spoon and supplies a useful warning that several other features became mainstream. The two formula dates remain medium-confidence hypotheses.
- Zappa official release page: https://www.zappa.com/releases/ship-arriving-too-late-save-drowning-witch/. Inspected release date and credits. Confirms 1982-05-03, the Valley Girl track, and Moon Zappa vocal credit. Does not independently establish lexical peaks or reproduce the lyrics.
- Emily Kendall, Britannica, *Yuppie* (updated 2026-10-01): https://www.britannica.com/topic/yuppie. Inspected article and chronology. Documents possible 1980 print appearance, 1984 prominence, pejoration, and the reported 1991 death declaration. Precise annual density is unmeasured.

## Temporal interpretation and limitations

Signatures represent an appropriation window in the named kairolect, not a claim about a term's invention or extinction. All fade dates are estimated. The CB window of 1973–1982 is a curation hypothesis around directly attested 1976 popularity. Its onset and fade need independent historical frequency evidence in a subsequent review; no observed early-1980s death is claimed. Some forms remain current in specialist use. These limitations are repeated in the accepted records so downstream tools cannot mistake the parameters for annual measurements.

The Valley window is strongest around 1982. TIME's report of rapid turnover is unusually useful inclusion evidence. Surf-derived formulas have narrower signatures because the source already treats them as displaced; later surf or entertainment reuse is not folded into this particular moment. The yuppie peak is a press-prominence anchor rather than a statistical frequency estimate.

The beginning of the axis has deliberately thin coverage. No words have been invented to fill 1970–1972; runtime should resolve to the nearest supported region and the coverage report should expose that interval.

## Deferred or excluded material

- CB ten-codes, transceiver terminology, and ordinary acknowledgment words were excluded as technical or too enduring. The word `break` is included only for its documented interruption/request/apology ritual within the public CB appropriation, not every ordinary occurrence of the verb.
- `good buddy` was excluded because the inspected modern glossary reports a later sexuality-related meaning. No such term is needed to satisfy the intended register.
- Generic disco words such as boogie and funky were not accepted solely for being associated with the 1970s. A dated genre is not automatically a dated lexical fad.
- General Valley discourse markers, including like and totally, were excluded because the inspected source states that features spread into mainstream speech. Ordinary adjectives rad and awesome likewise need stronger term-specific displacement evidence.
- TIME's gendered, sexuality-related, or disability-related labels were excluded. Its mention of a term is not sufficient acceptance evidence.
- `rolf`, `scarf-out`, and `cas` are promising staged candidates. The contemporary article attests them, but this batch prioritizes formulas serving the requested communicative functions and needs a second source for their specificity and replacement.
- Yuppification, yuppiedom, preppy formulas, quality circles, Theory Z, and zero-based budgeting remain unaccepted pending direct usage and displacement research. Expansions of yuppie are variants in one entry, not separate entries.

Several attempted Wikipedia, Smithsonian, scholarly-PDF, and encyclopedia requests returned 403, and guessed article addresses returned 404 or unrelated content. Those responses were not used as evidence. Search-result snippets were not promoted into citations.


## Original-resolution CB source correction

A second inspection of the full-resolution Gateway rear sleeve exposed transcription mistakes in the initial low-resolution reading. The accepted records now use the exact historical forms **clear shot**, **break for a short short**, **drop the hammer**, and **good truckin'**. The unattested clean shot, shortened break-for-a-short surface, and put-the-hammer-down construction were removed from the stored surface/variant inventory. Stable ASCII entry identifiers were retained so references do not break.

The sleeve does not print got ears. That entry now uses the current CB World question **got your ears on?**, with no reduced variant and an explicit **low-confidence** historical placement. The 1973/1976/1982 numerical signature is preserved as a hypothesis; there is no direct 1976 attestation of the complete question. This is the weakest dating record in the batch and remains a priority for source review.

The revised records also distinguish bare break's current operating-guidance attestation from the related historical breaker headword; distinguish the historical repeated-transmission sense of come on back from the current come-back response-invitation sense; and add the primary-supported come back and ratchet-jaw variants. Stack them eights means best regards, not the separate affectionate double-eighty-eights formula. Let the channel roll invites others to join the conversation. Good truckin' literally wishes safe driving; a figurative onward closing remains a wish and does not describe past work.

An additional primary source was inspected: *Chilton's CB Handbook: 40 Channel*, revised edition, https://archive.org/details/chiltons-cb-handbook-40-channel-guide-to-choosing-installing-and-operating-citiz. The public scan's OCR includes the title/copyright/cataloging page, introduction, and glossary pages 151–152. Although uploader metadata labels the item 1976, the scanned revised edition has copyright 1977, 1976 and 1977 cataloging, so the corpus citation records **1977**. Its introduction directly connects the public boom to the 1973–74 fuel crisis and 1974 truckers strike. Its glossary corroborates hammer down, Ears as being equipped with CB, and eyeball as visual contact. It therefore provides additional historical support for the acceleration and visual-contact senses, but does not establish the full ears-question chronology or an observed fade.

Existing context hints, speech-function corrections, minimal constructed examples and accepted statuses were preserved. Examples were changed only where their former surface was mis-transcribed. No constructed example has been relabeled as an attested quotation. All fade dates remain estimated; no evidence of a measured term-specific 1982 disappearance was introduced.

## Demographic context constraint

The yuppie entry now requires explicit lexical context: its own label or expansions, compatible urban/upwardly-mobile/status-conscious professional phrases, or a materialist person reference. Generic people, teams, work, requests, and praise do not qualify. The ambiguous adjective professional alone is deliberately not a trigger. This prevents unrelated intentions from acquiring a demographic identity solely to satisfy a sampled component; the hints constrain retrieval, not historical likelihood.

General Valley reactions and CB floor openings/closing formulas remain context-free. Existing conditional CB hints for response invitations, caller recognition, visual contact, route clearance and continuous talking were preserved. Good truckin' already had no context_terms, so no restriction was removed: as a conventional onward closing it may accompany a speech act without asserting that the recipient is presently driving. Its literal safe-travel gloss and the distinction between a closing wish and a description of past work remain unchanged. No dates or speech-function memberships changed.


## Speech-role and incompatibility metadata

The CB safe-travel wish good truckin', the fine-day wish, stack them eights, and the down/gone departure formula are now explicitly marked as closing usage. Only the three wishes acquire declining and apologizing compatibility: each can follow a complete refusal or apology as a separate farewell, never as a retrospective description of the action. The departure formula retains its previous functions because it makes a departure assertion. No sixteens record is present in this curated batch. Three additional examples are expressly constructed demonstrations of the separate closing role.

Drop the hammer excludes explicit `not` contexts to prevent an acceleration imperative from replacing a prohibition. Valley incredulity or disgust formulas kiss my tuna, I'm shurr, grody to the max, and gag me with a spoon exclude explicit polite, politely, respectful, respectfully, courteous and courteously wording. These exclusions preserve speech acts requested in the input; they do not add a user tone control or change the historical signatures.

Joanie now requires explicit outdatedness, fashion or trend context. Generic location or scheduling changes do not establish a fashion judgment. Its former generic constructed scheduling example was replaced with a clearly constructed out-of-fashion example; no attested usage was changed. Sources, source-attested excerpts, canonical forms, variants, temporal parameters and accepted statuses remain unchanged.
