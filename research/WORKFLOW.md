# Curation protocol

Candidates require a bounded cultural moment, attested vocabulary, and evidence
of rapid adoption followed by backlash or displacement. First-use evidence is
not peak evidence. Retrospective accounts can establish a moment but cannot
justify precise annual frequencies on their own. Estimated temporal parameters
are curation hypotheses, explicitly qualified in the rationale and confidence.

Researchers return schema-shaped records into staging. Review checks citations,
examples, chronology, membership, function suitability, duplicate variants and
inclusion criteria. Only accepted records enter the runtime artifact. Quotes
must be exact and sourced; constructed examples identify their status.

Low-confidence and culturally borderline candidates remain staged for review.
Communicative functions describe compatible complete speech acts, including
discourse openings or closing wishes that accompany the act. They do not license
changes to a marker's meaning. Optional context terms are lexical hints for senses
with semantic prerequisites; retrieval conditions the material pool on compatible
contexts and functions before choosing a kairolexicon at the sampled year. An
intent-specific temporal gap resolves to the nearest compatible support. Only
an entirely unrepresented intent broadens to the available last-resort pool.
Reports expose uncovered years and semantic concentrations. Targets are not
permission to pad the corpus.

Records with insufficient inclusion evidence remain in `corpus/candidates/` and
are validated separately with `tuus validate --candidates`; they never enter the
runtime bundle. Every example must contain a declared lexical form. Publication
dates are null when the cited webpage is undated; a dated cultural artifact can
be identified separately in the source note. Declared variants require source
support rather than inferred expansions.
